<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 2: Decide tombstone retention and Clerk session revocation</name>
  <files>None</files>
  <action>Pause and obtain the owner's explicit tombstone/retention and provider-revocation selection. Do not infer a legal duration, default tombstone form, or revocation sequence from the options alone.</action>
  <decision>After local anonymization, how long and in what form is the deleted Clerk subject denied re-provisioning, and when is the provider account/session revoked?</decision>
  <context>The API verifies Clerk-signed tokens networklessly. A copied bearer token can remain signature-valid until expiry even after browser sign-out. The local deletion transaction can also remove the raw external subject, accidentally allowing the same subject to provision a new account. The current sources provide no jurisdiction, legal retention duration, or erasure-hold rule. The owner must choose the privacy-minimizing tombstone form, retention rule, and provider-revocation sequence; the implementation must fail closed if the provider call fails.</context>
  <options>
    <option id="keyed-permanent-tombstone-local-first">
      <name>Keyed pseudonymous tombstone, local-first (Recommended)</name>
      <pros>Immediately denies re-provisioning while retaining no raw Clerk subject; provider revocation can be retried without restoring local access.</pros>
      <cons>Retains a persistent pseudonymous deny marker, so its classification and indefinite retention must be consciously accepted.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="time-bounded-tombstone">
      <name>Time-bounded tombstone through token expiry</name>
      <pros>Bounds retention more explicitly and can discard the deny marker after the accepted window.</pros>
      <cons>The owner must supply the exact duration and token-expiry assumptions; expiry mistakes could re-enable a deleted subject.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="provider-only-revocation">
      <name>Provider revocation without a local tombstone</name>
      <pros>Avoids a separate local deny record.</pros>
      <cons>Networkless verification cannot prove current Clerk session state; provider failure or an already-issued token can permit re-provisioning unless another durable deny mechanism exists.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select `keyed-permanent-tombstone-local-first`, `time-bounded-tombstone`, or `provider-only-revocation`; if selecting the time-bounded option, state the exact retention duration and whether the tombstone contains a raw or keyed subject. Also state whether provider revocation is attempted before or after the local transaction and how a failed provider call is surfaced.</resume-signal>
